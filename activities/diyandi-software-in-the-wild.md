### 1. User group
**Who are you designing for?**
Local residents and daily commuters (including students and workers).

**Why might this group need support during Diyandi?**
During the month-long festival, they still need to travel to school, work, or home efficiently despite the massive influx of tourists and major events blocking normal routes.

### 2. Situation or need
**What is this group trying to do during Diyandi?**
This group is trying to navigate daily traffic, locate active public transport routes, and avoid unexpected road closures caused by festival activities like street dancing and parades.

### 3. Problem or inconvenience
**What may make this task difficult, confusing, unsafe, slow, or inconvenient?**
Information about road closures and rerouted public utility jeepneys (PUJs) is usually posted as long text or static images on various Facebook pages. It is a reasonable assumption that a commuter already on the road cannot easily read these scattered posts, leading them to wait at closed terminals or get stuck in severe traffic jams.

### 4. Proposed digital solution
**What digital tool would you propose?**
A mobile-friendly "Diyandi Commute & Route Map" website.

**How would it help the intended users?**
It provides a live, interactive map showing which roads are currently closed for events and highlights the temporary alternative routes for local jeepneys and tricycles, removing the guesswork from commuting.

### 5. Things users can do
**Describe two specific actions that users could perform using your proposed system.**
1. A user can view a color-coded map showing current road closures and real-time heavy traffic areas.
2. A user can click on a specific jeepney route (e.g., Buru-un to City Proper) to see its temporary drop-off and pickup points during parade days.

### 6. Important qualities
**Quality 1:** Low-data and fast-loading
**Why does this matter to users?**
Mobile internet during peak festival days in Iligan can be slow due to network congestion. Commuters need the map to load instantly on basic smartphones without consuming heavy mobile data.

**Quality 2:** Easy to use (Visually clear)
**Why does this matter to users?**
Commuters are often walking or standing by a busy road. The system must use simple visual cues (like red lines for closed roads, green for open) so users can understand the situation at a quick glance without reading long paragraphs.

### 7. How to tell whether the solution helped
**How could you determine whether your proposed solution actually helped users?**
I could determine its success by measuring if task-completion time improves (e.g., tracking how quickly a user can locate an alternative terminal on the app). I could also ask users for feedback via a short pop-up survey to see if they successfully avoided traffic jams compared to previous festival years.
